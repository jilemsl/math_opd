\begin{frame}{Setup}

Selective OPD reduces the loss over a chosen subset $M$ of completion positions:
$\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{|M|}\sum_{t\in M} D_t$, with $D_t$ the student--teacher divergence at $t$.

\medskip
Published selectors pick $M$ from \textbf{model outputs} (student entropy, teacher--student KL):
a forward pass \emph{before} choosing. Here $M$ comes from a regex over the decoded text.

\vspace{0.6em}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
{\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}l>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{4.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{mask} & \textbf{selects} \\
\midrule
\texttt{math\_only} & interiors of \texttt{\$..\$}, \texttt{\$\$..\$\$}, \texttt{\textbackslash[..\textbackslash]}, plus digit/operator runs outside them \\
\texttt{text\_only} & the complement: everything else \\
\midrule
\texttt{random}  & a flat fraction $r$ of each rollout's tokens, drawn uniformly, redrawn every step \\
\texttt{entropy} & top-$k$ student entropy \dn{(needs logits)} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
{\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{quantity} & \textbf{definition} \\
\midrule
retention $r$ & selected / all completion tokens \\
 & (prompt and padding excluded) \\
$\mathrm{cov}(M)$ & $\sum_{t\in M}D_t \big/ \sum_t D_t$ \\
mass/token & $\mathrm{cov}(M)/r$; \; $1.0=$ average \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}

\medskip
\aside{Masks are built on \emph{characters}, then mapped to tokens via
\texttt{offset\_mapping}: Qwen3 splits \texttt{12} into \texttt{1},\texttt{2} and glues a
leading space onto the next token.}
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\vspace{0.6em}
\aside{$D_t$ is the per-token loss standing in for gradient magnitude --- the same proxy the prior work reports, not a per-parameter gradient norm.}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Table 3 --- matched-budget control: \emph{how many} vs \emph{which}}

\aside{Every masked arm changes two things at once: the \textbf{budget} (how many positions
receive gradient) and the \textbf{selection} (which ones). A flat-rate random mask holds the
first fixed and makes the second uninformative, so the two can be priced separately.}

\vspace{0.4em}
\begin{block}{\small flat-rate random mask at rate $r$}
\small At every optimizer step, for each rollout independently, draw
$\mathrm{round}(r\cdot n)$ of its $n$ completion tokens uniformly without replacement;
gradient flows only there. Redrawn each step, blind to token content.
\aside{Measured retention over 120 steps: 0.6399 against a 0.64 target.}
\end{block}

\vspace{0.5em}
\centering
{\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{arm} & \textbf{retention} & \textbf{mass/token} & \textbf{MATH500} \\
\midrule
\texttt{math\_only}           & 0.638 & \dn{0.55} & \up{0.7580 $\pm$ 0.0120} \\
\texttt{random} @0.64 budget  & 0.640 & 0.86      & 0.7337 $\pm$ 0.0081 \\
\texttt{vanilla}              & 1.000 & 1.00      & 0.7363 $\pm$ 0.0090 \\
\midrule
\emph{untrained student}      & ---   & ---       & \emph{0.6990} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}

\vspace{0.7em}
{\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{contrast} & \textbf{prices} & $\boldsymbol{\Delta}$ & $\boldsymbol{p}$ \\
\midrule
\texttt{random}@0.64 vs \texttt{vanilla}   & the budget alone    & $-0.0027$ & \dn{0.722} \\
\texttt{math\_only} vs \texttt{random}@0.64 & the selection alone & \up{$+0.0243$} & \up{0.052} \\
\texttt{math\_only} vs \texttt{vanilla}     & both together       & $+0.0217$ & 0.072 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}

\flushleft
\vspace{0.6em}
\textbf{The budget is free; the selection is the whole effect.}
Withholding gradient from 36\% of completion tokens \emph{at random} costs nothing
($p=0.722$). It decomposes exactly: $+0.0243+(-0.0027)=+0.0216$.

\medskip
\aside{Same protocol as Table 2 --- lr $1\times10^{-5}$ constant, 120 steps, 32 rollouts/step,
3 seeds, train DAPO-Math-17K (en), eval MATH500 $\times4$, Welch $t$ on seed means. $+0.0243$
clears the 3-seed minimum detectable difference of $0.018$; $+0.0217$ did not.}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Table 4 --- mass/token at a \emph{single} fixed budget}

The obvious objection to Table 2 is that its three arms differ in \emph{budget} as well as
selection, so the reversal could be an artifact of how many tokens each one trains on.
The matched-budget control removes that reading.

\vspace{0.8em}
\centering
{\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{selection, all at retention 0.64} & \textbf{mass/token} & \textbf{MATH500} \\
\midrule
\texttt{math\_only} \aside{(lowest mass)}   & \dn{0.55} & \up{0.7580 $\pm$ 0.0120} \\
\texttt{random}                              & 0.86      & 0.7337 $\pm$ 0.0081 \\
\texttt{entropy} top-$k$ \aside{(highest mass)} & \up{1.48} & \aside{not trained} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}

\flushleft
\vspace{0.9em}
\textbf{At one fixed budget, the lower-mass selection wins by $0.024$.} The metric
anti-predicts \emph{within} a matched budget, not only across arms that spend different
amounts --- which is the stronger form of the claim.

\medskip
\aside{\texttt{entropy} is budget-matched and implemented but was never trained to completion;
it is shown here only to place the mass axis. Training it is the one experiment that would
close this table.}

\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 9
\begin{frame}{Conclusion}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Gradient-mass coverage anti-predicts accuracy.} Across three arms spanning
        0.55--1.77 mass/token, the accuracy ordering is exactly reversed
        ($p=0.022$ between the extremes).
  \item The metric would have selected \texttt{text\_only} --- which does not beat the
        untrained student ($p=0.570$).
  \item \textbf{Why, most likely:} high divergence marks \emph{arbitrariness} as readily as
        information. Prose diverges because phrasing is underdetermined --- many wordings are
        correct, so the models disagree without either being wrong. Notation agrees because it
        is \emph{determined}. \aside{Inferred, not demonstrated.}
  \item \textbf{The budget is free; the selection is the effect.} At a matched budget of 0.64,
        a uniform random mask is indistinguishable from training on all tokens ($-0.003$,
        $p=0.722$) --- so 36\% of completion tokens can be dropped at no cost --- while
        \texttt{math\_only} beats that same random mask by $+0.024$ ($p=0.052$). All of
        \texttt{math\_only}'s advantage is \emph{which} tokens it keeps, not how many.
  \item Across learning rates the regex \textbf{matches} full-token OPD; it does not beat it
        outright. The learning rate moves accuracy 5$\times$ more than the mask does.
  \item The teacher-side compute saving is real and \textbf{capped at 11.6\%}.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.6em}
\begin{block}{Not established}
\small
One teacher--student pair (4.02\,B$\rightarrow$2.03\,B), one training set, one benchmark
carrying the result (MATH500; AMC23 at 40 problems and AIME at 30 cannot resolve $0.02$).
\texttt{kl} and \texttt{entropy} are built and budget-matched but never trained to completion,
so the top row of Table 4 is empty. Three seeds is fragile: one added seed moved a $p$ from
0.040 to 0.072, and the headline $p=0.052$ sits on the wrong side of the usual threshold.
\end{block}

\end{frame}
