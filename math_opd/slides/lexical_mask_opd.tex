\begin{frame}{Setup}

Selective OPD reduces the loss over a chosen subset $M$ of completion positions:
$\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{|M|}\sum_{t\in M} D_t$, with $D_t$ the student--teacher divergence at $t$.

\medskip
Published selectors pick $M$ from \textbf{model outputs} (student entropy, teacher--student KL):
a forward pass \emph{before} choosing. Here $M$ comes from a regex over the decoded text.

\vspace{0.6em}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
{\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}l>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{4.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{mask} & \textbf{selects} \\
\midrule
\texttt{math\_only} & interiors of \texttt{\$..\$}, \texttt{\$\$..\$\$}, \texttt{\textbackslash[..\textbackslash]}, plus digit/operator runs outside them \\
\texttt{text\_only} & the complement: everything else \\
\midrule
\texttt{random}  & a flat fraction $r$ of each rollout's tokens, drawn uniformly, redrawn every step \\
\texttt{entropy} & top-$k$ student entropy \dn{(needs logits)} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
{\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{quantity} & \textbf{definition} \\
\midrule
retention $r$ & selected / all completion tokens \\
 & (prompt and padding excluded) \\
$\mathrm{cov}(M)$ & $\sum_{t\in M}D_t \big/ \sum_t D_t$ \\
mass/token & $\mathrm{cov}(M)/r$; \; $1.0=$ average \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}

\medskip
\aside{Masks are built on \emph{characters}, then mapped to tokens via
\texttt{offset\_mapping}: Qwen3 splits \texttt{12} into \texttt{1},\texttt{2} and glues a
leading space onto the next token.}
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\vspace{0.6em}
\aside{$D_t$ is the per-token loss standing in for gradient magnitude --- the same proxy the prior work reports, not a per-parameter gradient norm.}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Table 3 --- two axes, priced separately}

\aside{Every masked arm changes two things at once: the \textbf{budget} (how many positions
receive gradient) and the \textbf{selection} (which ones). A flat-rate random mask holds the
first and makes the second uninformative, so they can be measured apart.}

\vspace{0.3em}
\begin{block}{\small flat-rate random mask at rate $r$}
\small At every optimizer step, for each rollout independently, draw
$\mathrm{round}(r\cdot n)$ of its $n$ completion tokens uniformly without replacement;
gradient flows only there. Redrawn each step, blind to token content.
\aside{Measured retention over 120 steps: 0.2000, 0.4099, 0.6399, 0.8501 against targets.}
\end{block}

\begin{frame}{Table 6 --- the floor: training on the tokens that carry \emph{no} loss}

\aside{\texttt{rkl\_min}: per rollout, keep the $\alpha n$ tokens with the \emph{smallest}
$D_t=\mathrm{KL}(\text{student}\,\|\,\text{teacher})$ --- the trainer's own loss. Budget-matched
to \texttt{math\_only}. This is the oracle floor of the mass axis; it needs both models' full
projections, so it is a diagnostic, never a method.}

\centering

\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{arm} & \textbf{mass/token} & \textbf{MATH500} & $\boldsymbol{\Delta}$ \textbf{vs} \texttt{rkl\_min} & $\boldsymbol{p}$ \\
\midrule
\texttt{math\_only}   & 0.55  & \up{0.7580 $\pm$ 0.0120} & $-0.0152$ & 0.149 \\
\texttt{rkl\_min}     & \dn{0.025} & \textbf{0.7428 $\pm$ 0.0067} & --- & --- \\
\texttt{vanilla}      & 1.00  & 0.7363 $\pm$ 0.0090 & $+0.0065$ & 0.376 \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}

\flushleft
\vspace{0.8em}
\textbf{It captures 1.6\% of the divergence, trains at a loss of $0.0006$ against
\texttt{vanilla}'s ${\sim}0.25$ --- roughly $400\times$ smaller --- and still edges out
full-token OPD.} All three contrasts are null at 3 seeds; the finding is that it works at all.

\medskip
\textbf{It also breaks the law.} The fit predicts $0.7732$; observed $0.7428$, residual
\dn{$-0.0304$} against $\pm0.007$ everywhere else. Refitting drops $r^2$ from $0.93$ to
$\mathbf{0.70}$. \textbf{The optimum is interior} --- near $0.55$, not at the floor.

\medskip
\aside{Adam's update is invariant to a global rescaling of the loss, so the collapsed loss scale
does not act as a reduced learning rate. We predicted it would. It did not.}

\end{frame}

\flushleft
\vspace{0.9em}
\textbf{At one fixed budget, the lower-mass selection wins by $0.024$.} The metric

\medskip
\textbf{In context.} OPD buys $+0.059$ over the untrained student. A budget-matched random

teacher--student divergence than the mask it beats.

\medskip
\aside{\texttt{entropy} is budget-matched and implemented but was never trained to completion;
it is shown here only to place the mass axis. Training it is the one experiment that would
close this table.}

\begin{frame}{Conclusion}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Gradient-mass coverage anti-predicts accuracy, as a law.} Over
        mass/token $0.55$--$1.77$, five arms at three different budgets fit
        $\text{acc}=0.7742-0.0395\,m$ with $r^2=0.93$, every residual under the seed SD.
        The slope is \dn{negative}.
  \item \textbf{The loss itself is nearly dispensable.} A mask keeping the tokens with the
        \emph{lowest} divergence captures 1.6\% of it, trains at ${\sim}400\times$ smaller loss,
        and still edges out full-token OPD. It also breaks the law ($r^2$ $0.93\to0.70$), so the
        optimum is interior, not at the floor.
  \item The metric would have selected \texttt{text\_only} --- which does not beat the
        untrained student ($p=0.570$).
  \item \textbf{Why, most likely:} high divergence marks \emph{arbitrariness} as readily as
        information. Prose diverges because phrasing is underdetermined --- many wordings are
        correct, so the models disagree without either being wrong. Notation agrees because it
        is \emph{determined}. \aside{Inferred, not demonstrated.}
  \item \textbf{The budget does nothing, at either rate tested.} Sweeping a uniform random
        mask from $r=0.20$ to $1.00$ moves accuracy by $0.0143$ in total --- non-monotone,
        inside seed noise, no rate significantly below full-token OPD --- and the matched-budget
        null replicates at 3e-6 ($-0.003$, $p=0.599$) as well as 1e-5 ($-0.003$, $p=0.722$).
        This is the robust result.
  \item \textbf{Selection matters only at an over-large learning rate.} \texttt{math\_only}
        beats a budget-matched random mask by $+0.024$ ($p=0.052$) at 1e-5 but by $-0.006$
        ($p=0.173$) at 3e-6. Every arm at either rate tops out at \textbf{0.758}: the mask
        recovers what a too-high rate loses and adds nothing to a tuned baseline.
  \item Across learning rates the regex \textbf{matches} full-token OPD; it does not beat it
        outright. The learning rate moves accuracy 5$\times$ more than the mask does.
  \item The teacher-side compute saving is real and \textbf{capped at 11.6\%}.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.6em}
\begin{block}{Not established}
\small
One teacher--student pair (4.02\,B$\rightarrow$2.03\,B), one training set, one benchmark
carrying the result. \texttt{kl} and \texttt{entropy} are built and budget-matched but never
trained to completion, so the top row of Table 7 is empty. Three seeds is fragile: one added
seed moved a $p$ from 0.040 to 0.072, and the $p=0.052$ at 1e-5 sits on the wrong side of the
usual threshold. \textbf{Two learning rates is not a curve} --- the selection effect may peak
between them, or be specific to 1e-5.
\end{block}

\end{frame}
